**A7 elevation.** Timelock pairs are the **only class in the entire candidate set that yields a genuine causal edge**: `CallScheduled` and `CallExecuted` share an explicit `bytes32 id`. Given D2 stripped the causal-edge claim from Candidate 1, M3 currently has **no located source of causal edges at all**. A7 is not "another class"; it is the missing ingredient for the M3 deliverable "explicit causal edges."

**Structural finding neither WR4 nor the critique surfaces (INFERRED — LayerZero v2 mechanics; CANON: upstream-spec class per `SOURCE_REGISTER`; unverified against a pinned commit — see the pinning requirement below):** a genuine *crosschain* causal edge on this route is available from **LayerZero channel identity**, and it is not a GUID lookup. Correction to my earlier text: the destination-side events carry **no GUID**. `PacketVerified(Origin origin, address receiver, bytes32 payloadHash)` and `PacketDelivered(Origin origin, address receiver)` expose only `Origin{srcEid, sender, nonce}`, `receiver`, and (for `PacketVerified`) `payloadHash`.

Specify the join as a **channel tuple**, not a GUID:

- **Destination side (observed):** `dstEid` = the destination endpoint's own local EID (a contract read, not a log field), plus `origin.srcEid`, `origin.sender`, `origin.nonce`, `receiver`.
- **Source side (observed):** `PacketSent(bytes encodedPayload, bytes options, address sendLibrary)`. Decode `encodedPayload`'s packet header to `{version, nonce, srcEid, sender, dstEid, receiver}`.
- **Join key:** the 5-tuple `(srcEid, sender, dstEid, receiver, nonce)`. This is the linking artifact satisfying `ENGINEERING_SPEC:739` — an authored protocol field present on both sides, not wall-clock proximity.
- **GUID is a validation step, not a join key.** The GUID is derivable from those same channel fields, so recomputing it from the destination tuple and comparing against the GUID carried in the source `encodedPayload` **corroborates** a join already made — it cannot establish one, because the destination never emits it. Likewise `payloadHash` on `PacketVerified` is a second independent corroborator against the source payload. A join that survives tuple match **and** GUID recomputation **and** payloadHash match is strong; a tuple match alone is the minimum and must be labelled as such.
- **Non-uniqueness caveat (INFERRED):** the tuple is unique per channel only if `nonce` is monotonic and non-reused for that `(srcEid, sender, dstEid, receiver)` pathway. Nonce semantics must be confirmed at the pinned commit before the join is asserted rather than proposed.

**Configuration changes on two chains have no causal edge; message deliveries do.** M3's "explicit causal edges for crosschain event bundles" should therefore be satisfied by a *message* bundle, not a *config* bundle. **Hard precondition: none of the above may enter a claim until the LayerZero-v2 commit SHA is pinned (my own G12) and the event signatures, header encoding, GUID derivation, and payloadHash composition are re-derived from that pinned source rather than from `main` HEAD or from my recollection.** Until then this is a proposed mechanism, not an established one.

## 4. Supersession design notes for W6

W6's acceptance requires "a reorged block is superseded, not silently replaced, and orphaned observations are marked rather than deleted" (`roadmap/work/W6-m1-scenario-fixtures.md:75-79`), against `ENGINEERING_SPEC:733,932` and `THREAT_MODEL:110`. **No supersession machinery exists today** — a repo-wide search of `lib/` finds only comments (`adapter.ts:230-233`, `identity/compare.ts:435`).

### 4.1 Mechanical constraint that dictates the file layout

`loadRecordingBytes` rejects duplicate `(chainId, method, params, providerId)` keys (OBSERVED `lib/aegis/chain/adapter.ts:158-160`). Therefore:

- Identity/state reads on **both branches can coexist in one bundle**, because reads are keyed by EIP-1898 `{blockHash, requireCanonical:true}` (`adapter.ts:236-240`) and the two branches have different hashes.
- `eth_getBlockByNumber [N,false]` on both branches **collides** and is rejected.

⇒ **The fixture must be a bundle *pair*, not a single bundle** — a pre-reorg capture and a post-reorg re-capture, joined by an explicit supersession record. This is not a workaround; it is the honest shape, and it rhymes with the house's own receipt convention (`supersedes: [EV-W4-R2]`, OBSERVED `roadmap/evidence/EV-W4-R3.md:15`) — the NTSB pattern: both exhibits retained, the later one supersedes in the open.

### 4.2 What the fixture must contain

1. **Both observations retained**, each with its own `envelopeSha256`. Never an in-place edit — a mutable-row UPDATE on an observation is evidence tampering with extra steps.
2. **Block-hash pinning everywhere.** Every read carries `{blockHash, requireCanonical:true}`. A log without a block-hash pin is a rumor.
3. **The common ancestor, as data.** Both branch heads must record the *same* `parentHash` P, and P's own block record must be in the fixture. **The common ancestor is the exhibit that makes the supersession claim falsifiable** — without it you cannot distinguish a reorg from two unrelated chains. (CANON: The Graph's rollback-to-common-ancestor playbook.) `PinnedBlock` already carries `parentHash` (`selection.ts:11-18`), and `engine.ts:63-67` hashes it into `rawResultHash` while stripping `finality` — so **lineage disagreement already surfaces as a quorum conflict today**, and same-number/different-hash already yields `block_hash_mismatch` → `conflict` (`quorum.ts:101-109`). W6 adds retention, not detection.
4. **Superseded-by linkage**, explicit and additive.
5. **A derived artifact that must survive — via an external supersession record.** My earlier formulation ("retains its original hash while gaining a `supersededEvidence` limitation") is **impossible and is withdrawn**: `limitations` are normalized *inside* the hashed payload (`lib/aegis/report/canonical.ts:568`, `sortLimitations` within `normalizeReport`, consumed by `canonicalBytes`/`reportHash` at `:652-659`). Adding a limitation necessarily changes the report hash. Retention and annotation cannot both live in one artifact.

   **Design chosen: (i) the branch-A report stays byte-immutable; supersession is an EXTERNAL additive record that points at it.** Rationale, and why not (ii): after a reorg the branch-A reads are absent under `requireCanonical:true`, so the observation yields `timeout` (`engine.ts:71-75`) and **there may be no successor report at all** — a design that expresses supersession only through a new report cannot annotate the common case. The external record works whether or not a successor exists.

   Shape: `SupersessionRecord` (§4.4) gains `affectedArtifacts` — ~~`Array<{ reportHash: sha256, citedObservationIds: sha256[] }>`~~ **superseded by the normative schema in §4.4** (artifacts are identified by `artifactKind` + content hash, never by a bare field name that could be re-aimed at a mutable pointer). Resolution rule: any surface rendering a report **must** query the supersession index by that report's hash and display the superseding record alongside it. The annotation lives at the *presentation and index* layer, never inside the sealed payload.

   When a successor report **does** exist, it additionally carries the house's back-reference (`supersedes:`, as in `roadmap/evidence/EV-W4-R3.md:15`) — a subordinate convention layered on (i), not an alternative to it.

   Cache invalidation is already mechanical (`ENGINEERING_SPEC:930` keys include block hash); byte-immutability plus an external pointer is the honesty requirement.
6. **The orphaned branch's post-reorg reads must be ABSENT from the post-image bundle.** Under `requireCanonical:true` a canonical node refuses an orphaned hash. Omission yields `status:"timeout"` = missing evidence (OBSERVED `engine.ts:71-75`, `identity/observe.ts:163-170`), which is the correct answer. **Schema note:** `RecordedResponse` has no error variant, so "provider refused as noncanonical" and "we never captured this" both collapse to `timeout`. Honest but imprecise; W6 should consider an explicit recorded-error variant so `quorum.ts`'s `malformed` path is reachable from recordings.
7. **Finality-class honesty guard — the hard one.** `PinnedBlock.finality` admits `"finalized"` (`selection.ts:17`). **A fixture that reorgs a block labelled `finalized` on chain 1 fabricates protocol history** — exactly W6's own stated hazard (`:105-107`). Ethereum finalized blocks do not reorg absent a >1/3 slashable fault. The chain-1 reorg fixture must orphan an `unconfirmed` block at depth 1-2.

### 4.3 OP-specific — this answers critique C5

OP finality is three-class, derived from L1: `unsafe` (sequencer-published) → `safe` (batch on L1) → `finalized` (that L1 block finalized), ~20-30 min (OBSERVED `WR3/provider-matrix.md:60-62` `[P-O1]`). Therefore, for the OP fixture:

- The honest OP failure is **not an L1-style fork-choice reorg**; it is **unsafe-head replacement** by the sequencer. Orphan an `unconfirmed` block. A `safe` block can also be orphaned if the L1 block carrying its batch reorgs — a second, deeper scenario worth a separate fixture.
- **Design defect I am raising against `selection.ts` (INFERRED, needs owner review).** QuickNode declares no `finalized`/`safe` tags on OP (OBSERVED `lib/aegis/chain/providers.ts:37`; `WR3:77` `[P-Q2]`), so Aegis's OP boundary falls to `confirmationDepthTarget` (`selection.ts:85-94`) — an L2 **block-depth** count. On OP, block depth does not bound reorg risk: unsafe-head replacement is bounded by *L1 batch posting*, not by L2 block count. **A confirmation-depth policy on OP measures the wrong quantity** — depth-N superstition, asserted without citing a finality mechanism. The correct fallback derives OP finality from L1: pin the highest L2 block whose batch originates in an L1-finalized block. WR3's own probe already proposes the cross-check (`provider-matrix.md:245-247`). This is worth an `INS-*` capture; I do not edit repo files.

### 4.4 Proposed module — extend, never fork

`lib/aegis/chain/lineage.ts`, matching the house style of `quorum.ts`/`selection.ts` (pure evaluator, no I/O, no clock, typed `ChainError`):

```ts
interface SupersessionRecord {
  schemaVersion: "1";                                // frozen encoding version
  contentHash: `sha256:${string}`;                   // excluded from its own preimage
  superseded:   { observationId: `sha256:${string}`; chainId: number;
                  block: { number: string; hash: string; parentHash: string } };
  supersededBy: { /* same shape */ };
  commonAncestor: { number: string; hash: string };
  depth: string;                                    // canonical decimal
  reasonCode: "reorg_block_hash_changed" | "reorg_lineage_diverged";
  finalityAtObservation: "unconfirmed" | "confirmations" | "safe";  // never "finalized"
  detectedAt: { number: string; hash: string };      // a CHAIN position, not a clock;
                                                     // display metadata, never a sort key
  affectedArtifacts: Array<{
    artifactKind: "assurance_report" | "recording_bundle" | "evidence_ref";
    artifactHash: `sha256:${string}`;                // content hash ONLY — never a path,
                                                     // filename, id, or mutable pointer
    citedObservationIds: Array<`sha256:${string}`>;  // sorted, deduped set
  }>;                                                // sorted by (artifactKind, artifactHash)
}
```

No new identifier class is invented: `observationId` / `citedObservationIds` are the existing content-addressed ids — `EvidenceRef.id` (OBSERVED `ENGINEERING_SPEC:212-213`) for report-cited observations, `envelopeSha256` (OBSERVED `adapter.ts:23,149-152`) for recorded-response identity. `recording_bundle` refers to the `bundleContentHash` proposed in `custody-chain.md:53-55` — **not yet shipped**; until it lands, that `artifactKind` is unusable and must be rejected rather than defaulted.

**Canonical bytes — domain-separated, and why.**

```
supersessionCanonicalBytes(r) =
    utf8("aegis/supersession-record/v1")            // domain tag
  ‖ 0x00                                             // separator: JCS output can never
                                                     // contain a raw NUL byte
  ‖ utf8( jcsSerialize( normalizeSupersessionRecord(r) ) )

supersessionRecordHash(r) = "sha256:" + hex( sha256( supersessionCanonicalBytes(r) ) )
```

`normalizeSupersessionRecord`: delete `contentHash`; sort `affectedArtifacts` by `(artifactKind, artifactHash)`; sort and dedupe each `citedObservationIds`; reject a duplicate `artifactHash` within one kind. This is `normalizeManifest`'s exact pattern (OBSERVED `trust.ts:90-110`), reused rather than reinvented.

Why the tag, stated honestly. OBSERVED: every shipped hash surface in this repo digests **untagged** JCS bytes — `reportHash` (`canonical.ts:652-660`), `manifestContentHash` (`trust.ts:112-118`), `rawResponseSha256`/`envelopeSha256` (`adapter.ts:91-92,143,150`), and `ENGINEERING_SPEC:846` states the report formula unprefixed. The `sha256:` token is appended **after** the digest (`adapter.ts:91-92`) — an output label, not a preimage tag. So four artifact classes already share one unprefixed sha256 space. The tag does **not** retro-fix them: those hashes are frozen identity (`custody-chain.md:92`) and re-basing them is an owner decision with a migration cost this ruling does not propose. What the tag buys is narrow and real — **a fifth class cannot join that space**, so a SupersessionRecord's bytes can never be presented as a report's bytes under the same hash function. (CANON: EIP-712 `domainSeparator`; TLS 1.3 signature context strings; NIST SP 800-185 cSHAKE function-name/customization strings. Domain separation is the boring, settled answer to cross-type preimage confusion.)

**Anchoring — tamper-evident *relative order* comes from the register, never from the record.** (Round-4 correction: the round-3 heading read "ordering and no-backdating come from the register," which overclaimed against this section's own bound three paragraphs below and reproduced exactly the error the round-3 body was written to fix. Withdrawn. The register establishes relative order and asserts nothing about wall-clock time.)

Every field inside a SupersessionRecord is self-declared, so nothing inside it can order it. Anchor each record in an append-only, hash-chained register:

```
entry_n     = { recordHash: "sha256:…", prevEntryHash: "sha256:…" | null,
                seq?: "<canonical decimal>" }   // OPTIONAL, display only — see R14
entryHash_n = "sha256:" + hex( sha256( utf8("aegis/supersession-register/v1") ‖ 0x00
                                       ‖ utf8( jcsSerialize(entry_n) ) ) )
```

- `data/supersessions/REGISTER`, committed; head `entryHash_N` mirrored into deployment trust policy as `approvedSupersessionRegisterHead` — the *shape* of `ManifestTrustPolicy.approvedHashes` (OBSERVED `trust.ts:78-81`) and of the digest register at `custody-chain.md:63-64`, reused as a pattern only. The state vocabulary is defined below on its own terms, not borrowed.
- **Ordering has exactly one source: the walk.** ~~"`seq` plus the `prevEntryHash` chain"~~ — **withdrawn (round 4): two sources with no conflict rule is not an ordering, it is an ambiguity, and it left a coherently rehashed nonmonotonic register able to support contradictory orders while staying approved-reachable.** `position(e)` is the hop count from genesis to `e` along `prevEntryHash`, walked backwards from `approvedSupersessionRegisterHead`. Nothing else orders a record — not `seq`, not `detectedAt`, not file line order, not block number.
  - ~~**Genesis rule** and **Single-path rule** — prose for "the walk must terminate at genesis" and "a single unbroken continuation past the head."~~ — **withdrawn (round 5): adjectives are not an algorithm, and they left `position(e)` undefined for exactly the entries R14 constrains.** A backward walk from the approved head cannot reach an entry that comes *after* it, so pending `seq` had nothing to equal. Replaced by one executable procedure, `loadRegister(E, H)` — `E` the parsed entry set, `H = approvedSupersessionRegisterHead`. Every step is O(|E|), deterministic, and reads no clock:

    1. **Verify every entry hash.** For each `e ∈ E` recompute `entryHash(e)` over the domain-separated bytes above. An entry is **identified by its computed hash**, never by a declared id; two entries computing the same hash are the same entry. Any parse / I-JSON / domain failure → `register_entry_malformed`, whole file refused.
    2. **Require the approved head to be present.** Exactly one `e ∈ E` must satisfy `entryHash(e) === H`; call it `e_H`. Zero matches → `register_head_absent`. That is a **refusal, not an empty register**: an approved head naming an entry we do not hold is a *missing* register, and reading it as "nothing to apply" is precisely the fail-open this design exists to prevent.
    3. **Walk backwards, uniquely, to genesis.** From `e_H` follow `prevEntryHash`. At each hop the predecessor must exist in `E` and be unique by hash — absent → `register_dangling_prev`; already visited → `register_cycle`. Stop when `prevEntryHash === null`. Reverse the visit list to get the **validated prefix** `P = [P₀ … P_k]`, `P_k = e_H`. `P₀` is genesis and is the **only** entry in `E` permitted to carry `prevEntryHash: null`; a second null-prev entry anywhere in `E` → `register_genesis_invalid`.
    4. **Scan forward past the head, outdegree ≤ 1.** On `R = E \ P` define `succ(y) = { x ∈ R : x.prevEntryHash === entryHash(y) }`. From `y = e_H`, require `|succ(y)| ≤ 1` at every step. `|succ(y)| ≥ 2` is a **fork** — two competing futures for one approved past — → `register_fork`, whole file refused; a fork is never silently resolved by picking a side. Follow the unique successor to exhaustion, yielding the **pending tail** `T = [t₁ … t_m]`.
    5. **Reject anything unvisited.** `E` must equal `P ∪ T` exactly. Any `e ∈ E \ (P ∪ T)` is an orphan — a discarded branch — → `register_orphan_entry`, whole file refused. Silently skipping an orphan is the behaviour this design exists to prevent.
    6. **Position, now total.** `position(P_i) = i` for `0 ≤ i ≤ k`; `position(t_j) = (k + 1) + (j − 1) = k + j`. **Pending position = validated-prefix length + forward hop count** — the round-5 answer to the backward-walk gap: pending positions come from step 4's *forward* scan anchored on the validated prefix, so `position` is a **total** function on `E` and R14 is well defined for pending entries. `position` never consults `seq`, `detectedAt`, file line order, or block number.
    7. **`seq`, if present, must agree — and never arbitrates.** For every `e ∈ E` carrying `seq`, `seq(e)` must be the canonical decimal of `position(e)`; otherwise `register_seq_desync`, whole file refused. The conflict rule stated flatly: **the walk always wins, and a disagreement never resolves in `seq`'s favour — it refuses.** Omitting `seq` entirely is the safer authoring choice and is explicitly permitted.
  - **Bound, stated alongside the fix so it does not overclaim (round 4).** These rules catch an *incoherent* rewrite. A **coherent** wholesale rewrite — re-chained, re-hashed, `seq` renumbered to match, head re-approved in the same commit — is self-consistent and no internal rule can refuse it. That residue is bounded exactly where the paragraph below bounds it: detectable in git history, prevented only by the external anchor.
- **Record admissibility — its own state vocabulary, not the manifest's.** ~~"A record not reachable from the approved head yields `unknown` with a typed reason — the untrusted-manifest path reused (`trust.ts:214-219`)"~~ — **withdrawn (round 4): wrong citation and wrong state class.** OBSERVED corrections: `trust.ts:213-219` is the **load-time content-hash integrity** path (recompute `manifestContentHash(raw)`; mismatch **throws** `ManifestError("integrity_mismatch","/contentHash")`). Approved-set evaluation is `evaluateTrust` at **`trust.ts:266-278`**, returning `state: "trusted" | "untrusted"` with `reasonCodes ["approved_hash"] | ["manifest_hash_not_approved"]` — a **`PolicyTrustState`** (`ENGINEERING_SPEC:166`, enum-guarded `canonical.ts:434`), a different type from `VerificationState` (`ENGINEERING_SPEC:150-156`). And an untrusted manifest emits **no** `unknown` verifications at all: `loaded` is non-null only when trusted (`trust.ts:299-308`), `targetsOf(null)` returns `[]` (`lib/aegis/surfaces/engine.ts:293-296`), so nothing is verified (`surfaces/engine.ts:199-201`). **Round-5 note:** this document cites two different files as `engine.ts` — `lib/aegis/surfaces/engine.ts` here, and `lib/aegis/chain/engine.ts` in §4.2 (`:63-67`, `:71-75`, both re-verified accurate). Bare `engine.ts` is ambiguous; prefer the qualified path. The supersession mapping therefore stands alone, in two levels:
  1. **Register-trust state — about the record.** `registerTrust: { state: "anchored" | "unanchored" | "invalid"; registerHead; recordHash; reasonCodes }` — the *shape* of `PolicyTrustBlock` (`trust.ts:285-291`), a new vocabulary, no shared code path.
     - recomputed `supersessionRecordHash` ≠ embedded `contentHash` → **`invalid`**, `["integrity_mismatch"]`, and the record is **never evaluated for anchoring** (the "an invalid policy is not evaluated" discipline, `trust.ts:293-296`).
     - valid, but its entry is not reached by the walk from `approvedSupersessionRegisterHead` → **`unanchored`**, `["record_not_in_approved_register"]`. A record in the pending tail past the head is also `unanchored`, with the distinguishable reason `["record_pending_register_approval"]` — same admissibility, different diagnosis.
     - valid and on the walk → **`anchored`**, `["approved_register_position"]`.
  2. **Selector consumption — the round-5 correction, and the serious one.** ~~"for any verification newly evaluated whose evidence cites the affected observation, `VerificationState` = `unknown` with reason `supersession_unanchored`"~~ — **withdrawn: it identified "the affected observation" from the untrusted record's own `affectedArtifacts`, i.e. from the very bytes whose integrity check had just failed.** A forged record could therefore name any observations it liked and force them to `unknown` with no approval anywhere in the path. Rule (**R15**): `affectedArtifacts` — the only field that aims a supersession at other artifacts — is consumed **exclusively** from records whose `registerTrust.state === "anchored"`. In every other state it is not parsed, not indexed, not rendered, and not echoed into any limitation.
     - **`invalid`** (recomputed `supersessionRecordHash` ≠ declared `contentHash`): contributes **nothing**. No supersession, no selector, no limitation quoting its content, no observation id echoed anywhere. The only emittable fact is *that a record was refused*, keyed by its **computed** hash — derived from bytes we hold, never asserted by them — plus the typed code. Every byte of such a record is attacker-chosen: quoting `citedObservationIds` out of it hands an unapproved party a targeting primitive, and quoting any other field puts an unauthenticated string on an evidence surface.
     - **`unanchored` / `record_not_in_approved_register`** (hash valid, not on the approved walk): contributes **nothing aimed**. Integrity is not approval. A well-formed forged record is exactly as dangerous as a malformed one for aiming, and strictly easier to produce. It may appear in an operator-facing "present but unapproved" index by computed hash and reason code only.
     - **`unanchored` / `record_pending_register_approval`** (hash valid, on the pending tail `T`): the one case that **may warn and may never aim**, stated exactly. MAY raise an operator-facing notice that a pending supersession exists at `position(t_j)`, identified by computed record hash. MUST NOT contribute selectors; MUST NOT change any `VerificationState`; MUST NOT annotate any report surface; MUST NOT enter a coverage denominator. Its bytes are chained, so a forger must at least append coherently — but position is not approval, and approval is the human gate. A pending record that could downgrade results would let anyone with append access degrade verdicts without review.
     - ⇒ **The only path from a record to a result-state change is: valid content hash → entry on the approved walk → `anchored` → selectors consumed.** Everything else is inert with respect to results.
     - **Why this is not fail-open — the obvious objection answered.** "A real reorg with no approved record now reads as `pass`" is false, and the safety net is deliberately *not* the record: a reorged branch's reads fail under `requireCanonical:true` and become missing evidence, `status:"timeout"` (OBSERVED `lib/aegis/chain/engine.ts:71-75`, `lib/aegis/identity/observe.ts:163-170`), while same-number/different-hash across providers already yields `block_hash_mismatch` → `conflict` (OBSERVED `lib/aegis/chain/quorum.ts:101-109`). Detection is grounded in the pinned reads, which no unapproved input can aim; the record supplies **retention and narrative, not detection**. A detection mechanism steerable by unapproved bytes would be the vulnerability, not the feature.
  3. **Register-wide structural failure — what "refuses the whole file" means downstream.** Steps 1-7 and R12-R14 refuse the *file*; that must carry exactly one defined meaning. The loader **throws** a typed `SupersessionRegisterError{ code, path, detail }` and never returns a partial register — the house loader idiom (OBSERVED `trust.ts:213-219` throws `ManifestError`; `adapter.ts:143-153` throws at integrity mismatch). The engine catches once at the boundary and emits a **register-level** state, never a per-record verdict: `registerLoad: { state: "loaded" | "refused"; registerHead; reasonCodes: [<typed code>] }`. On `refused`:
     - every record's `registerTrust.state` is **`unanchored`** with `["register_refused", <code>]` — not `invalid` (their own hashes may well be fine; the *register* failed) and never `anchored`;
     - **no selectors are consumed from any record** (R15), so the degradation **cannot be aimed** — which is the point: a refused register contains no trustworthy targeting information;
     - the degradation is therefore **blanket and structural**: any newly evaluated verification citing an observation whose boundary finality is **not** `finalized` goes `unknown` with reason `supersession_register_refused`. Verifications citing only `finalized` boundaries are unaffected, because supersession of a finalized block is forbidden outright (R2);
     - a payload `limitation` names the register head hash and the typed code — **nothing read out of the refused file**;
     - sealed reports are untouched.
  4. **Never retroactive.** All three levels attach to the *evaluation* and the *timeline rendering*, never to an already-sealed report; a sealed report's bytes and `reportHash` are immutable (test 1).
- Deleting, reordering, or inserting an entry breaks the chain at that point and at every entry after it.

**What this proves and what it does not (NTSB discipline — the correction the reviewer demanded).**
- PROVES: tamper-evident **relative order**. Record R was appended after every entry reachable through its `prevEntryHash`.
- DOES NOT PROVE: absolute wall-clock time. A hash chain has no clock. An owner who rewrites the register and the approved head in one commit produces a self-consistent forgery — **detectable in git history, not prevented** (the same bound drawn at `custody-chain.md:56`).
- ⇒ The honest claim at this design's strength is **"tamper-evident relative order," not "no-backdating."** No-backdating against the owner needs the external anchor already ranked at `custody-chain.md:59-60`: minimally a fast-forward-only ref mirrored to two remotes, properly an independent timestamp anchor (OpenTimestamps/Rekor). **Until that anchor lands, no surface, test name, or acceptance sentence may say a supersession "could not have been backdated."** ANTI-CANON: a bare content hash asserted as chronology.

Rules, each one a test:

- **R1** supersession requires same `(chainId, number)` with different hash, or a descendant whose recorded `parentHash` chain no longer contains the superseded hash.
- **R2** `finalityAtObservation === "finalized"` → throw `finalized_reorg_unsupported`. On chain 1 a fixture asserting this fabricates history; if it ever occurs live it is a consensus fault deserving its own loud reason code, never silent modelling.
- **R3** common ancestor absent or not an ancestor of both by recorded `parentHash` → `lineage_not_established`; outcome `unknown`, never an asserted supersession.
- **R4** no deletion — superseded observations stay resolvable; the record is purely additive.
- **R5** acyclic and totally ordered per chain: A←B←C; asserting C←A throws `supersession_cycle`.
- **R6** a reorg *back* to an earlier branch is a **new** observation with a new envelope hash superseding the current head. "Un-supersede by deletion" is forbidden.
- ~~**R7** ordering key is `(chainId, blockNumber, capturedAt-of-superseding)`~~ — **withdrawn (round 3): `capturedAt` is declared by the very artifact being ordered, so it cannot establish chronology any more than a bare hash can.** ~~**R7′** ordering key is register position — `(seq, prevEntryHash-chain)`.~~ — **also withdrawn (round 4): naming two sources without a conflict rule is an ambiguity, not an ordering.** Replaced by **R7″**: the ordering key is `position(e)` — **the hop count along `prevEntryHash` from genesis, walking back from `approvedSupersessionRegisterHead`, and that walk is the sole source.** `seq` is display-only and never a tiebreaker (R14); `blockNumber` alone remains forbidden (two branches share a number); `detectedAt`/`capturedAt` are display metadata that MUST NOT be used as a sort key. - **R9** `affectedArtifacts` entries identify artifacts **by content hash only**. A path, filename, report id, or any other mutable pointer in that position is a typed schema failure (`invalid_artifact_reference`): a pointer can be re-aimed after the fact, which is precisely the tampering this record exists to make impossible.

Never a silent apply. ~~"…and any newly evaluated verification citing the affected observation is `unknown`/`supersession_unanchored`."~~ — **withdrawn (round 5): that clause aimed a downgrade using the untrusted record's own selectors; see R15.** (Round-4 note retained: the *record* takes a register-trust state, the *result* takes a `VerificationState`; they are different type classes and conflating them was the round-3 error.)

- **R13** the register is a single path: every entry lies on the walk from the approved head or on its single unbroken pending continuation. Anything else is `register_orphan_entry` and refuses the whole file.
- **R14** `seq`, if present, must equal `position(e)` — now total over `P ∪ T` by `loadRegister` step 6, so the rule binds pending entries too; disagreement is `register_seq_desync` and refuses the file. A `seq` conflict is never resolved in `seq`'s favour — the walk wins by construction, and the disagreement is reported as tamper evidence.
- **R15** `affectedArtifacts` selectors are consumed **only** from `anchored` records. An `invalid` record contributes nothing at all — not even a limitation quoting its content; an unapproved-but-valid record contributes nothing aimed; a pending record may warn (notice by computed hash and position) and may never aim. The only path from a record to a result-state change is valid hash → approved walk → `anchored`.
- **R16** a structurally refused register **throws** a typed `SupersessionRegisterError` — never a partial load — and maps to a **blanket, unaimed** degradation: every record `unanchored`/`register_refused`, and any newly evaluated verification citing a non-`finalized` boundary goes `unknown`/`supersession_register_refused`. A refused register may never produce a *targeted* downgrade, because it holds no trustworthy targeting information.
